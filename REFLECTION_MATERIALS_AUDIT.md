Reflection 03 can explain how risk assessment changed the way the author makes security decisions. The reviewer provides enough material to develop each of these ideas:

- Identifying something valuable precedes deciding how to protect it. Information collections, their owners and their dependencies matter alongside the systems holding them.
- An important asset does not automatically have the highest assessed risk. A scenario also needs a threat, vulnerability, consequences and consideration of existing safeguards.
- Likelihood and impact help prioritize attention, while uncertainty limits how confidently a score can be interpreted.
- Risk evaluation adds judgment about acceptability and authority. A numerical ranking alone does not decide what the organization should accept.
- Feasibility and residual risk make security an ongoing responsibility. Treatment is followed by verification and review.

The following qualifications affect how the material should be used:

1. **Reported implementation and demonstrated effectiveness are different claims.** The PDF attributes controls to interviews and consultations. A reflection can say the report documented or staff reported them. Claims about measured effectiveness require the relevant test results or operational records.

2. **Retain uncertainty where the report retains it.** Backup coverage, restoration evidence, account ownership, patching and some access-lifecycle details remain incompletely established. The controls table sometimes says restoration has not been tested, while F-02 says no restore test was reported. The supported reflective point is that restoration effectiveness was not established by the assessment. Likewise, an inventory unknown to one respondent should remain an inventory confirmation issue.

3. **A routine practice does not establish the frequency of a harmful outcome.** The likelihood justifications for link sharing and inventory problems rely heavily on the existence of routine practices. Their risk ratings are the team's qualitative estimates, rather than measured incident probabilities. When mentioning file sharing, preserve the role of the actual permissions and account ownership in determining exposure.

4. **Keep modeled figures labeled.** The report uses estimates for some accounts, personnel, equipment and infrastructure. The earlier [inventory's evidence rules](<../Final Term Project - Risk Management/Assessment/02 - Asset Inventory and Valuation.md>) explain this distinction in detail. These values support an academic scenario and should not become claims about a measured institutional census.

5. **Recommended improvements are future actions.** Section VIII explicitly says none of the proposed controls has been implemented and expected results are not measured outcomes. A reflection can describe what the group recommended, how it prioritized those proposals and what verification would require. It cannot claim that the project already reduced the organization's actual risk.

6. **Use the reviewer’s numerical examples cautiously.** Its likelihood table pairs illustrative annual probabilities with recurrence descriptions that are not numerically aligned, such as 25% in the next year alongside approximately once in ten years. Use the concepts and the project's explicitly qualitative scale without presenting these illustrative values as measured probabilities. The reviewer's ethical discussion also supports acknowledging uncertain inputs honestly.
